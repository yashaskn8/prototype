# Operation 21 — accuracy root-cause investigation

**Verdict: `MULTIPLE_CONFIRMED_BOTTLENECKS`.** The Operation 20 fine-tuned BGE checkpoint reproduces **16/43 = 37.21% exact labeled Hit@1**. Its labeled passage exists in the local eligible and authorized corpus for all 43 relevance questions. The dominant measured retrieval problem is **ranking**, compounded by an inadequately reviewed training set and an unreliable, partly unanswerable benchmark. A general **95%+ Hit@1 or grounded-answer accuracy claim is unsupported**. No model was retrained or promoted, no labels were changed, and no live system was deployed.

## Scope and reproducibility

This audit used the local SQLite snapshot, local Chroma collections, the saved Operation 20 checkpoint, and benchmark SHA-256 `f516a183d510a275de5856cb9d26eefc055181223d28cc8630db914613508dda`. It did not access authoritative live MySQL or original attachment binaries. Reproduction and measurement scripts are in `tools/op21_*.py`. Query-level records, scores, authorization checks, and review notes are in ignored `private_training_data/op21_accuracy_audit/`; in particular `incorrect_query_diagnostics.jsonl` covers **every one of the 27 original Hit@1 misses** with the question, asset, expected chunk, original top ten and cosine scores, candidate-stage ranks, and a provisional mechanism. These are research diagnostics, not human relevance adjudications.

The saved Operation 20 experiment had 31 training rows, 6 validation rows, one epoch, eight optimizer steps, learning rate `2e-5`, batch size 4, and a 256-token cap. `ml_models/op20_run_report.json` confirms 207 vectors from 57 eligible documents. Replaying its saved Chroma lookup produced **16/43 Hit@1, 27/43 Hit@5, 33/43 Hit@10, and 40/43 Hit@50**. The original report's `fingerprint_matches_report: false` compares two different hash algorithms: the full training-file digest `3abb5dae…` and the collection fingerprint `aca57cce…`. Both reproduce their respective recorded values; that flag is not evidence of a damaged Operation 20 checkpoint.

## 1. Why measured accuracy is low

The following are confirmed observations or strongly evidenced failure mechanisms. The last two are integrity and architecture constraints; their contribution to the 16/43 score has **not** been isolated.

**Training negative inspection.** There are 37 assigned negatives; all are in-tenant, 28 share a document with their positive, and 20 chunk IDs used as a negative for one query are a positive for another. The latter is a false-negative *risk*, not proof of an incorrect label. Base BGE gave the assigned positive a larger cosine score than its assigned negative in all 37 pairs; mean margin 0.124, and 12/37 margins exceed 0.15. These negatives may be easy or merely lexically different. `TR-PL-04` is a concrete validation concern: its question asks for ISO 46 fluid/filter scheduling while its assigned “Monthly Maintenance” passage discusses battery terminals, linkages, and tires. Human review is required before using validation loss to select a model.

**Source relationships.** Local SQLite has 166 documents and 208 chunks, of which 57 documents/207 chunks meet index eligibility. Tenant 9 contributes 9 documents/69 chunks; tenant 6 contributes 151 total documents and 131 eligible chunks. The nine tenant-9 attachment checksums are in the hardcoded `core/services/customer_isolation.py` registry; the local `SourceProvenanceRecord` table is empty. Thus nine registered attachments, 57 eligible documents across all tenants, 17 distinct training-positive documents, and 207 eligible chunks count different things. None proves that all 57 documents are authenticated live company attachments. Training spans 11 product/asset families but ranges from one row each for Autopick, Paper Shredder, and KTM to seven for Split AC. The observed family coverage is inadequate for a broad industrial claim.

## 2. Where labeled positives are lost

| Stage | Labeled positives present | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| SQLite, index eligibility, and authorized applicable candidate pool | 43/43 each | No observed loss at these stages in this snapshot. Authorized pools contain 8–23 chunks per question. |
| Saved Operation 20 Chroma top 10 / top 50 | 33/43 / 40/43 | Original pure-dense lookup, replayed without modification. |
| Fair in-memory BGE FT top 5 / top 8 / top 10 / top 20 / top 23 | 30/43 / 34/43 / 35/43 / 41/43 / 43/43 | Same corpus and authorized pool for every model. This is an exact cosine ranking, not Chroma approximate search. |
| Fair lexical top 10 / top 50 | 38/43 / 43/43 | BM25 plus character n-grams; rankings differ from BGE. |
| Dense ∪ lexical top 50 | 43/43 | Oracle pool coverage; no cross-encoder ran. |
| Final saved Operation 20 top 1 | 16/43 | Its pure dense rank, without fusion or reranking. |

**Oracle bounds:** on this small, asset-constrained, single-labeled set a perfect reranker restricted to the fair FT top 8 could reach at most 34/43 (79.1%) exact-label Hit@1; top 10 at most 35/43 (81.4%); top 20 at most 41/43 (95.35%); top 23 or all authorized chunks at most 43/43. These are *upper bounds conditional on the labels*, not forecasts. Since every authorized pool has at most 23 chunks, the 43/43 top-50 result is mechanically easy and says little about a larger production corpus. A true technical answerability or label-quality ceiling cannot be computed until subject-matter reviewers resolve the invalid/ambiguous passages and check original source authenticity. Relevance for other valid passages must be multi-label.

The 256-token cap is not the cause in this snapshot: of 207 eligible BGE passage texts, 101 tokenize to at most 64 tokens, 64 to 65–128, and 42 to 129–256; maximum 255, none truncated. The BGE query prefix is consistent between training and serving (`core/ml/embeddings.py:51,313`), and cosine distance is converted as `1 - distance` for Chroma's cosine space. Headings/body, rather than truncation, need controlled review. The nine Overview hash mismatches prevent assuming every existing vector matches the current passage representation.

## 3. Controlled experiment results

Each row below uses all **43 relevance questions**, the same 207 eligible chunks, identical authorized/applicable candidate sets and single-ID metric code. Local models were offline; no production collection was rebuilt. P95 is warm query encoding plus in-memory authorized ranking over all 64 cases; it excludes database and Chroma I/O and **must not be compared directly** with Operation 20's endpoint p95. Model weights and process RAM were not benchmarked for every configuration; the saved FT checkpoint occupies 134 MB on disk.

| Configuration | Hit@1 | Hit@5 | MRR@10 | nDCG@10 | P95 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fair MiniLM baseline | 24/43 = 0.5581 | 35/43 = 0.8140 | 0.6727 | 0.7139 | 11.97 |
| Fair base BGE-small | 14/43 = 0.3256 | 30/43 = 0.6977 | 0.4815 | 0.5562 | 21.63 |
| Fair Operation 20 BGE FT | 16/43 = 0.3721 | 30/43 = 0.6977 | 0.5146 | 0.5866 | 20.50 |
| BM25 plus character n-grams | 16/43 = 0.3721 | 28/43 = 0.6512 | 0.5121 | 0.5992 | 6.51* |
| Fixed RRF, BGE FT + lexical | 15/43 = 0.3488 | 33/43 = 0.7674 | 0.5236 | 0.6004 | not timed end to end |
| Offline cross-encoder on FT top 20 | 18/43 = 0.4186 | 28/43 = 0.6512 | 0.5340 | 0.5997 | 498.76† |
| Reranked hybrid | not run | not run | not run | not run | not run |

\* Lexical p95 is scoring/ranking only. † Cross-encoder p95 is *additional scoring of the authorized dense pool* and excludes dense, DB, and Chroma latency. RRF used dense and lexical top 50, weights 0.55/0.45, `k=60` without fitting on holdout. It raises Hit@5 and slightly raises MRR/nDCG, but **reduces Hit@1** by one case; it is not a successful Hit@1 optimization. A separate six-question weak-validation ablation gave dense 4/6 Hit@1, dense with generic overviews removed 4/6, and lexical 5/6. Read-only chunking ablations on the same six cases mapped child passages back to their original parent chunk: original 4/6 Hit@1 (207 vectors), paragraph blocks 4/6 (216), procedure blocks 3/6 (334), and short overlapping line children 4/6 (1,094). The extra vectors and embedding work brought no measured gain; shorter children require parent context for any answer. This validation sample is too small and weakly labeled to select a serving change.

The offline cross-encoder was the locally cached `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` (91 MB weight file), which differs from the serving constant's spelling `ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2`; it was not wired into serving. We scored each already-authorized FT candidate once and evaluated fixed top-8, top-10, top-20, and top-50 prefixes without tuning. All four depths gave **18/43 Hit@1**. Top-8 gave 30/43 Hit@5, top-10 29/43, top-20/50 28/43; deeper exposure allowed irrelevant candidates to displace useful passages. The six weak-validation questions remained 4/6 Hit@1 at every depth, with p95 scoring 413 ms. This fixed-model holdout inspection is exploratory and cannot validate a 95% strategy. `tools/op21_rerank_audit.py` records every rank.

Category Hit@1 (FT / MiniLM): exact error codes 1/6 / 5/6; general symptoms 6/8 / 6/8; misspelled 1/7 / 3/7; model traps 0/6 / 2/6; model ambiguity 2/4 / 3/4; multi-symptom 4/6 / 3/6; technician 2/4 / 1/4; equipment crosstalk 0/2 / 1/2. By equipment, FT is 4/11 on Split AC, 3/8 on VRF, 3/9 on Ductable AC, 0/2 on Voltcore, and only one to three cases for other families (full counts in `summary.json`). These are diagnostic slices, not stable population estimates.

Wilson 95% intervals illustrate uncertainty: FT Hit@1 16/43 has interval **24.4–52.1%**; MiniLM 24/43 **41.1–69.6%**; even 43/43 would have lower bound about **91.8%**. One extra hit moves the 43-case proportion by 2.33 percentage points. Hit@1 of at least 95% requires at least 41/43 labels and would still not certify 95% production reliability.

The original report's 21/21 abstention accuracy concerns a separate set of 21 security/unsupported cases. In `core/ml/evaluation.py:86-95`, an abstention is counted correct only when the ranked list is empty. This is largely a scope/candidate-empty test; it does not measure whether a generated technical answer abstains appropriately when retrieved context exists. Grounded-answer accuracy was **not measured** here. Neither correct equipment/document selection nor physical-action safety is established by an exact chunk-ID match.

## 4. Engineering program toward 95%+

The measured upper bound and the unreliable labels make a numeric expected gain unjustified. The ranges below are **recoverable-case bounds**, not predicted improvements; every proposed change needs a reviewed development set and then one untouched evaluation set.

| Priority | Intervention and measured basis | Possible exact-label effect on current 43 (upper bound, not forecast) | Gate and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review sources and relevance. Adjudicate the 10+ suspect technical labels, six alternate top passages, and all safety/identifier cases. Record all valid passages and answerability. | Published Hit@1 may rise or fall; no defensible numeric forecast. | Two independent technical reviewers; retain original holdout and create a new adjudicated version without favoring model output. |
| 2 | Fix index identity and stale metadata checks (code included). Build only versioned, isolated indexes for experiments; inspect the nine Overview mismatches and older company FT collection. | Unknown; mismatch was not shown to cause the Op20 score. | No serving promotion; dry-run reconciliation first. Hash/metadata checks add small lookup work; full re-embedding costs time and disk. |
| 3 | Develop exact model/code matching and bounded typo normalization within the authorized pool. Examine `EVAL-ERR-01/03`, `TRAP-*`, and `XTLK-*`; reject unsupported codes. | At most 27/43 currently wrong labels recoverable; actual subset must be measured. | Require source-supported exact identifiers; no metadata-only override, no LLM-invented specifications. Lexical rank latency p95 6.51 ms in memory. |
| 4 | Test authorized dense + sparse candidate union and calibrated ranker on reviewed development data. Fixed RRF is not sufficient. Check top 10/20 pools and top 1 separately. | From current FT rank, top-10 oracle permits at most +19/43; top-20 at most +25/43, counting questionable labels. | Local permitted reranker only, record p95 and RAM, compare with MiniLM. Current reranker sees only first 8, which caps this oracle at 34/43. |
| 5 | Experiment with technical child chunks retaining parent context and warning/table boundaries; contrast original, paragraph, procedure, and answer-bearing child variants one at a time. | Unknown; six apparent errors may be neighboring valid passages. | Re-chunk only copied development corpus; measure same reviewed relevance and safety, not just smaller text. Overview-removal alone showed no gain on six validation cases. |
| 6 | Collect reviewed, family-balanced examples before another versioned fine-tune. Compare base BGE, fair MiniLM, and any policy-permitted stronger model on identical indexes. | Unknown; 31 weak rows do not estimate full-domain gains. | Group by source/manual and equipment at split time; keep final holdout untouched; never auto-promote. |

**Success criteria:** pre-register separate Hit@1, Hit@5, MRR@10, nDCG@10, candidate recall, correct equipment/document selection, abstention, grounded-answer correctness, unsafe-answer rate, and p95 latency. Report both exact-ID and human-adjudicated semantic relevance. Preserve tenant/customer/asset authorization before retrieval and after ranking. Run blinded failure review and a source/document-separated holdout from real complaint distribution. Passing a 43-case replay alone is insufficient.

## 5. Next fine-tuning strategy (after data repair)

Plan for **50–100 newly and independently reviewed answerable query/passage examples per equipment family** as an initial pilot, roughly **550–1,100 across the 11 currently represented families**, with at least three to five reviewed hard-negative judgments per example and several valid positives where the manual supports them. This is a collection target, **not a power calculation or promise of 95%**; expand families and examples based on learning curves and confidence intervals. Reserve 20–30% additional separately sampled unanswerable/safety/ambiguous cases for routing and abstention evaluation, not as forced positive passages. Require original source checksum, equipment model, revision, customer scope, reviewer identity, and answer-bearing span for each approved example.

Coverage should deliberately include: exact fault-code and model strings; real natural-language complaints; authentic misspellings; multi-symptom cases; model-specific and product-specific distinctions; cross-equipment confounders; missing/unsupported answers; and safety-critical questions where a generic manual must defer to approved procedure. Preserve identifiers during normalization. Review each negative for genuine irrelevance, especially same-document neighboring sections and near-duplicate manuals. Use in-batch negative de-duplication, multiple positives, and manual/source-group-aware batches to avoid turning valid passages into negatives. Compare the current MultipleNegativesRankingLoss baseline with a reviewed hard-negative triplet or ranking loss; search modest learning rates (for example `5e-6` to `2e-5`), one to three epochs, and early stopping **only on a reviewed development split**. These ranges are hypotheses, not established optima. Evaluate per family and query type, then once on a sealed, source-separated test set. Do not retrain on the existing 31 rows expecting unexplained gains.

## 6. Integrity fixes and remaining limits

This branch adds: checkpoint-aware model caching and collection fingerprint rejection (`core/ml/embeddings.py`); complete expected vector metadata plus dry-run mismatch detection (`core/ml/indexer.py`); BGE vector-to-current-chunk validation before serving (`core/services/retriever.py`); and a single verified benchmark identity through evaluation (`core/ml/evaluate_benchmark.py`). These fail closed or report discrepancies; **no collection was repaired or promoted**. The old company FT collection now correctly rejects its mismatched checkpoint. Read-only reconciliation reports the noncompany base BGE collection as 138 expected/137 present, two missing, one orphan, and 136 metadata mismatches against the new full manifest; this does not affect the isolated 207-chunk fair comparison. Legacy MiniLM vectors lack enough metadata to prove chunk identity in the same way; further repair needs a versioned rebuild.

Tests: 26 targeted and 16 broader SQLite test executions pass, including four new integrity tests; `git diff --check` and Python compilation pass. The in-memory baseline latency comparison excludes database, Chroma, reranker, LLM, and end-to-end answer generation; the separately reported reranker cost is additional. No answer correctness, safety, live MySQL provenance, original attachment integrity, production traffic distribution, or broad 95% attainment can be inferred from it.
